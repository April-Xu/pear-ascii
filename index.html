00 / THE FINDING

A general model learns
to act in the physical world.

An open 27B reasoning model learns robot control through reinforcement learning entirely in simulation. It observes, reasons and outputs the next arm pose. The learned ability transfers to unfamiliar instructions, different robots and real-world tasks.

01 / METHOD

Observe. Reason. Act. Repeat.

Like a computer-use agent, but the output moves a robot. Camera views and arm state enter the context; the model emits an end-effector pose. Inverse kinematics and a controller execute it.

06 / OPEN QUESTIONS
Not the end of the experiment.
[01] Scale & coverage
Training covers two simulators and a limited task set, starting from a 27B model. Real-world results cover 30 trials, not broad deployment reliability.
[02] Reasoning latency
Long reasoning before each move remains slow. At 747 tokens/s, an average xhigh round still takes about 4.3 seconds; some chains reach 32k tokens.
[03] Prompts & control
Performance depends on detailed embodiment and task descriptions. The model outputs one pose at a time, so accuracy and efficiency also depend on inverse kinematics and the controller.
